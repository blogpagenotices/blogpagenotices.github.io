If you started following GTA 6 recently, it's easy to get lost among delays, leaks, and announcements that have piled up over nearly three years. Here's everything, laid out from the beginning, so you understand exactly how we got to where we are today.

## 2023: the announcement that broke the internet

It all started in December 2023, when Rockstar released the first GTA 6 trailer earlier than planned, after a leak forced the company to move up the publication. That first look confirmed what rumors had been hinting at for years: a return to Vice City, now set within the fictional state of Leonida, and a protagonist duo instead of a single character.

## 2024 and most of 2025: strategic silence

For nearly two years, Rockstar maintained almost total discipline: no concrete date, no gameplay, just occasional confirmations that development was moving forward. Take-Two, the parent company, kept saying the launch would come "at some point in the fiscal year" — an elegant way of committing to nothing specific while expectation grew on its own, fueled almost entirely by community rumors and investor analysis.

## The first delay: from fall 2025 to May 2026

The first semi-official date that circulated was fall 2025. That window broke when Rockstar announced the first real delay, moving launch to May 2026. The official reason: they needed extra time to polish a game of this scale. It was a disappointment for fans, but a relatively expected one — few analysts really believed in the 2025 date to begin with.

## The second delay: the date that finally stuck

What did come as a surprise was a second delay, this time from May to **November 19, 2026**, accompanied by a statement where Rockstar explicitly apologized for extending the wait. This second postponement landed amid protests from laid-off former employees, which for a moment raised doubts about whether the issue was purely about quality or something deeper within the company. Rockstar insisted it was solely about finishing the game to the level fans expected, and so far, this date has held firm.

## Pre-orders open and the first official cover art

On June 25, 2026, Rockstar opened official pre-orders for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, alongside the reveal of the game's official cover art — the action-scene collage you already know if you read our [guide to everything confirmed so far](/en/posts/1-todo-lo-que-sabemos-gta6/). By that point we already knew the date, the price of both editions, and that the PC version remained unannounced.

## The CyberLeek storm: August gets complicated

Just weeks before the big official event, a group identifying itself as **CyberLeek** unleashed the biggest leak since the 2022 hack. Between August 18 and 25, more than nine gameplay videos circulated, along with an alleged full map and a set of demands wrapped in a manifesto that, as we analyzed at the time, had [more than one crack in its story of genuine protest](/en/posts/8-cyberleek-token-cripto-dudas/). The situation escalated into a real legal case, with Take-Two filing subpoenas against Microsoft, Discord, X, Google, and GitHub to identify the person responsible.

## Rockstar breaks 25 years of silence

On August 26, one day before the planned event, Rockstar did something it almost never does: publicly comment on a leak. They called the situation "heartbreaking" for their team, apologized for the extended wait, and almost simultaneously confirmed new official material — as if trying to regain control of the conversation right before their own event. You can read the full breakdown of that moment in our [article on Rockstar's response](/en/posts/10-rockstar-rompe-silencio-filtraciones/).

## The Extended Look: 26 minutes that changed everything

August 27 brought the event the whole community had been waiting for: **"Grand Theft Auto VI: An Extended Look,"** streamed exclusively on Netflix six hours before hitting YouTube. Twenty-six minutes of real gameplay showed a combat system directly compared to Max Payne 3, a Lucia capable of defending herself hand-to-hand without needing weapons, and a wealth of additional details — fashion, pets, streamers woven into the narrative — that we broke down in our articles on the [gameplay recap](/en/posts/11-extended-look-resumen-gameplay/) and the [details almost nobody talked about](/en/posts/14-detalles-extra-revelados-hoy/).

## A record even Netflix didn't expect

The numbers that followed confirmed this wasn't just passing enthusiasm. The Extended Look became the single most-watched piece of content in Netflix's history, outperforming movie premieres. App traffic rose 125%, and the video has already racked up millions of views on YouTube. All of this is documented with exact figures in our [analysis of the Netflix record](/en/posts/15-extended-look-record-netflix-mapa/).

## Pre-orders surge, and consoles sell out

The commercial impact didn't take long to show: pre-orders rose **436% in one day** and **572% cumulative over two days**, according to Sensor Tower data. The effect was strong enough that PS5 Pro stock started running out, with resale prices reaching as high as $1,300 — something we covered in more detail in our article on the [console shortage](/en/posts/17-ps5-pro-agotada-reventa/).

## The whole industry reacts

The most unexpected effect of all this didn't happen within GTA 6 itself, but in competitors' release calendars. Entire studios shifted their dates to avoid a head-on collision with Rockstar in November, creating an unusually packed September full of major launches — the full subject of our [article on GTA 6's effect on the industry](/en/posts/18-septiembre-vacio-industria-evita-gta6/).

## Where things stand today

With the release date locked in, official marketing already underway, and a community more active than ever, everything suggests that the road to November 19 will keep generating headline after headline. We'll keep updating this timeline as new things happen — bookmark this article if you want the full recap of how we got here always within reach.
