After nearly three years of announcements, delays, leaks, and finally an Extended Look that answered a huge number of questions, there's one topic Rockstar still hasn't said a single word about: the online mode. No date, no confirmation it even exists, not a single detail. For a company that built a huge part of its financial empire on GTA Online, that silence stands out — and deserves a deeper look than we've given it so far.

## The only thing we know for certain: nothing

When Rockstar announced GTA 6's pricing and editions, it referred exclusively to the single-player experience. There wasn't a single mention of a new multiplayer component. That doesn't mean it won't exist — it would be strange for a series that revolutionized games-as-a-service to abandon that formula entirely — but it does confirm that, as of November 19, the only guaranteed thing is Jason and Lucia's campaign.

## The theory: a journalist connects the dots

Journalist Paul Tassi put forward a theory that's generated plenty of conversation in the community: the new GTA Online could be much further off than most people assume, possibly by years. His argument isn't idle speculation — it rests on a pattern Rockstar has followed before, one worth revisiting calmly.

## The precedent almost nobody remembers: the 2013 chaos

When GTA Online launched in October 2013, barely two weeks after GTA V, the rollout was a technical disaster. Servers crashed, players lost saved progress, and the multiplayer experience took weeks to stabilize. Rockstar learned that lesson the hard way: launching a massive online mode at the same time as a giant open-world game is an enormously risky combination, even for a studio with all the resources in the world.

## The pattern repeated with Red Dead Redemption 2

When Red Dead Redemption 2 arrived in October 2018, Rockstar didn't repeat the mistake. Red Dead Online launched as a beta a month later, in November, and didn't reach its full version until well into 2019 — nearly a full year's gap from the base game. It was a deliberate decision: let the single-player campaign have its own moment, and develop the online component with more care, without the pressure of a simultaneous launch.

## Why GTA 6 could follow the exact same path

If Rockstar applies the same logic — and everything suggests it will, given how meticulous the company has been with every aspect of this launch — it would make sense for the new online mode, if it exists, to arrive months or even years after November 19. The scale of Leonida's map, which we already confirmed is several times bigger than Red Dead Redemption 2's in our [map size analysis](/en/posts/15-extended-look-record-netflix-mapa/), only makes the task of building a stable multiplayer experience on top of that same world more complex.

## The business reason nobody mentions: the current GTA Online still works

Here's the point many people overlook: **the current GTA Online is still a money-making machine** thirteen years after launch. Take-Two has reported for years that this online mode remains a relevant revenue source for the company, far beyond what anyone would have predicted back in 2013. From a purely commercial standpoint, Rockstar has no real urgency to rush out a replacement — every extra month GTA Online keeps generating revenue is, literally, money that didn't depend on GTA 6 already being finished.

## Does this disappoint players?

It's understandable that part of the community wants a new online mode from day one — thirteen years is a long time playing the same online base, no matter how many updates it's received. But it's worth remembering that Jason and Lucia's campaign, the one actually confirmed for November, is probably the most ambitious experience Rockstar has ever built, judging by everything shown in the [Extended Look](/en/posts/11-extended-look-resumen-gameplay/). The absence of online at launch might end up feeling less like a gap and more like a deliberate choice of focus.

## What the community is speculating in the meantime

Across forums and social media, theories range from a surprise announcement shortly after launch, to the possibility that Rockstar keeps the current GTA Online running indefinitely while building something entirely new for GTA 6 from scratch, without rushing. None of these theories have official confirmation — and they probably won't until Rockstar decides the timing is right, following the exact same careful communication pattern it's kept throughout this entire cycle.

## Our take

Rockstar's silence on the online mode doesn't look like an oversight or an attempt to hide bad news. Seen in the context of its own track record — the 2013 chaos, the patience with Red Dead Online, and a current revenue source that's still working perfectly well without needing an immediate replacement — it makes more sense to read it as a calculated decision. If the priority is giving Jason and Lucia the launch they deserve, leaving online for later might, paradoxically, be the smartest move Rockstar can make.

We'll keep following this story closely — if Rockstar drops even the smallest hint about the future of online, you'll read it here first.
