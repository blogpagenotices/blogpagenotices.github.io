## The billboard nobody expected to see at a train station

Earlier this year, some Norwegians passing through train stations in Oslo ran into a pretty unusual ad: **"GTA 6 comes out in 9 months ;)"**. No further context, no giant logo, just that line and that wink. One Reddit user summed it up better than anyone: it was, in their words, one of the funniest ways they'd seen a game advertised.

## The challenge is exactly what it sounds like

Behind the billboard was **Komplett**, a Norwegian electronics chain, running a promotion that sounds like a joke but is completely real: have a baby exactly on **November 19, 2026** — GTA 6's official launch date — and the store gives you a free copy of the game. Not a day early, not a day late. The exact day.

Do the math: nine months before November 19 lands, almost perfectly, right around Valentine's Day. The campaign couldn't have picked a more fitting moment.

## "This is actually not a joke"

Faced with a wave of people assuming it was pure marketing with no substance behind it, Komplett confirmed it directly on Instagram, making clear this was **"actually not a joke"** and that any baby born on that exact day would earn their parents a free copy. The promotion is limited to Norwegian citizens who can prove the birth took place within the country — so, unfortunately, you can't just claim a free game from anywhere in the world.

## This has happened before

If it sounds like an unprecedented level of chaos, it isn't entirely: **Bethesda did something very similar when it launched Skyrim**, promising free copies to babies born on release day. It seems that in the video game industry, when a launch is big enough, someone somewhere decides that tying it to the arrival of a new human being is a solid marketing idea.

## The community didn't miss the irony

Reactions came in quickly, and many pointed exactly where you'd expect: to the fact that a first-time parent probably won't have a single free minute to play anything in those early months. One user summed it up by calling it **"pretty funny"** to imagine a brand-new parent having spare time for a new game. Others, more practically, pointed out the obvious: however much GTA 6 ends up costing, a kid is still considerably more expensive.

## The real countdown is almost over

Here's the curious part: as we write this, we're just over **seven weeks out from November 19**. Any baby conceived around that Valentine's window we mentioned should be arriving, or about to, almost in sync with the game's actual launch. Somewhere in Norway, there are parents counting down the days right now with a very particular motivation attached.

## In the end, is it worth it?

Probably not — between diapers, sleepless nights, and a total life adjustment, it's hard to picture anyone firing up the console during those first few months of parenthood. But as a marketing strategy, credit where it's due: few campaigns have gotten this much conversation out of a launch while using, literally, zero material from the game itself.
