## The Extended Look wasn't the only thing that dropped today

While everyone was talking about the gameplay shown on Netflix, Rockstar used the same day to release a huge amount of additional information — some through new official screenshots, some through an exclusive interview with Dazed magazine. Here's what got overshadowed by the noise of the main event.

## A fashion system designed by a real stylist

One of the more curious details: Rockstar has had **Jillian Carr**, a Miami-based stylist, working with them since 2023 to help build what the company itself calls a "fashion ecosystem" inside the game. According to Carr, in Vice City "appearances carry significant weight" and trend cycles turn over quickly — almost as if the game's wardrobe operates on its own real-fashion logic, not just as decorative cosmetics.

## You can pet the dogs

A small detail that instantly generated a lot of affection online: it was confirmed you'll be able to **pet dogs** in the game. It's the kind of mechanic that doesn't change anything about core gameplay, but tends to create a disproportionate emotional connection with the community — it wouldn't be the first time a game goes viral for exactly this.

## Streamers as part of the narrative

GTA has always been a satire of American culture, and this time it's diving fully into live streaming. Rockstar confirmed streamers will have a role within the game's narrative — something new for the mainline series, which had touched on social media before but not the specific ecosystem of live content creators.

## Snapmatic is back

The fictional social network that debuted in GTA V returns in GTA 6. Lucia mentions it directly at one point in the Extended Look, confirming that little nod to Instagram remains part of the game's world.

## Why these details matter

None of these elements on their own is going to sell the game — but together they paint a picture of a much more alive and detailed world than a single trailer can convey. It's the kind of information that usually gets lost among the bigger headlines, but that ends up defining what it actually feels like to spend hours inside Leonida.
