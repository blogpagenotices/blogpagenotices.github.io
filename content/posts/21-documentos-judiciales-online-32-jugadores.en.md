## A direct update to our previous theory

Just a few days ago we analyzed why Rockstar's silence on GTA 6's online mode [might be a deliberate decision rather than an oversight](/en/posts/20-futuro-gta-online-teoria/). Now there's a development that updates that story: for the first time, there's documentary evidence that this online mode is genuinely being discussed internally.

## What the court documents reveal

The information came to light through documents filed in an employment tribunal, related to the dismissal of a group of Rockstar employees. In those documents, the company referenced internal discussions about a **"confidential 32-player online format"** for GTA 6. It's the first time Rockstar has publicly mentioned, even if indirectly and unintentionally, the existence of a multiplayer component for the game.

## What does "32 players" mean?

The number is significant because it marks a possible continuity with the current GTA Online, whose standard sessions also tend to handle up to 32 players per server, with some variation depending on platform and specific mode. Rockstar using that same figure in its internal discussions could suggest continuity with the formula it already knows, rather than a radical redesign of simultaneous player capacity. Even so, there's still no public official confirmation, so all of this should be treated as one more piece of the puzzle, not an announcement.

## The context that gives this news more weight

Former Rockstar programmer Matt Freedman recently revealed a historical detail that connects directly to this story: during GTA Online's original development in 2013, the mode was treated internally as **"an afterthought,"** with very little confidence from the team. Freedman said early testers found barely four hours of playable content, which reinforced internal skepticism about whether the mode would work at all. GTA Online, as we know today, turned out to be the exact opposite: one of the longest-running revenue sources in the industry. If history repeats itself, the fact that Rockstar is discussing a specific player format for GTA 6 could be an early sign of something that, once again, ends up exceeding internal expectations.

## Sales projections also went up

While this was coming to light, analyst firm **Piper Sandler** updated its sales projection for GTA 6's launch, raising it to roughly **48 million copies**. If it holds, that figure would place the game among the biggest launches in entertainment history, not just gaming.

## The community is already feeling it

Interest on social media also spiked in recent weeks: activity in the GTA 6 Reddit community **nearly quadrupled** in just a few weeks, according to the latest data. It's one more sign that, far from cooling off after the Extended Look, conversation around the game keeps growing.

## A curious detail: who gets to play first

A leaked rollout map suggests GTA 6 will go live at midnight local time in each region of the world, following each area's own time zone. According to that map, **New Zealand would be the first place on the planet** able to play it, with an estimated unlock around 11:00 UTC on November 18 — technically before November 19 even begins in much of the rest of the world.

## What this changes (and what it doesn't)

This news doesn't confirm a date for the online mode, nor does it fully settle the theory that it could take time to arrive after the main campaign's launch. What it does do is give real weight to something that was, until now, pure speculation: GTA 6's online mode exists, at least as a concept in active development, and we'll likely know more in the coming months as November 19 approaches.
