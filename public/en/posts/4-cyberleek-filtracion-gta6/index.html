CyberLeek: who is the hacker behind the biggest GTA 6 leak since 2022

A group calling itself CyberLeek leaked gameplay footage and the full map of GTA 6 days before the official Netflix presentation. Here is what we know.

The second-biggest leak since 2022#

Just days before the official GTA 6 presentation on Netflix, a group identifying itself as CyberLeek leaked gameplay videos and what it claims is the complete map of the game. According to specialized outlets, this is the most significant leak since the infamous 2022 hack, when an attacker known as “teapotuberhacker” published around 90 videos from an early build of the game — an incident Rockstar officially confirmed as a real intrusion into its servers.

What exactly leaked?#

Since August 18, 2026, several clips have been circulating showing Jason Duval, one of the protagonists, playing street basketball, driving on a highway, and beating another driver after a collision. The videos also reveal mechanics details: a fuel gauge, a wanted level system going up to six stars, knife melee combat, and taser use. Alongside the clips, an alleged full map of Leonida also began circulating.

Not just a leak for the sake of it: there’s a manifesto#

Unlike previous leaks, CyberLeek published a manifesto explaining its motives. The group demands three things from Rockstar and the industry at large: don’t sell digital pre-orders without also offering a physical edition, don’t sell fake downloadable content for the single-player mode, and preserve that mode’s content long-term. According to reports, the protest is directly aimed at Take-Two’s decision to launch GTA 6 without a physical disc, something we covered in our article on everything we know so far.

Is the leak real?#

This is where caution is warranted. Several outlets point out that part of the material circulating under this leak’s name may be AI-generated, mixed in with clips that do appear authentic — causing confusion even among specialized journalists. An insider known as NateTheHate claimed the genuine clips are over a year old, meaning they wouldn’t reflect the game’s current state. Rockstar, for its part, has issued copyright takedown notices for some of the material, but has not publicly confirmed or denied the leaks’ authenticity.

The strangest twist: now it charges for votes#

In an unusual turn, CyberLeek is trying to monetize the leaks: it published a poll on its own website where users can vote on which clip to leak next, paying with a cryptocurrency the group created itself. According to reports, at least one person has already paid the equivalent of $80 to take part.

Does this affect the launch?#

For now, there’s no indication the leak will change Rockstar’s plans. The release date remains set for November 19, 2026, and the company hasn’t signaled any change to “Grand Theft Auto VI: An Extended Look,” scheduled for August 27 on Netflix (with a YouTube premiere six hours later).
